St. Housing Pete Analytics

Using parcel-level data the consumer tools don't expose to answer one question: which St. Petersburg house is actually a good buy?

Role
Solo (data pipeline, modeling, UI)
Timeline
Personal project · 2026
Stack
DuckDB · dbt · FastAPI · Next.js + MapLibre
Repo ↗

The question

There are many factors to consider when buying a house in Florida: Hurricanes, storm surge, sinkholes, mold issues, termites, insurance costs, and even market conditions. When I decided to buy a house, I found the current platforms for doing an analysis were inadequate, specifically for assessing storm surge risk. Additionally, the flood maps are dated, and FEMA will be redrawing them soon. After a lot of thought, I came up with this criteria for my house:

  1. Block construction
  2. Slab foundation
  3. FEMA Zone X (non-flood zone)
  4. In either Evacuation Zone E or Non-Evac
  5. At least 20 feet above sea level, 25-30 is ideal
  6. Not a flip

Using the current tools to house hunt (Zillow, Redfin, Realtor), I found that I couldn't easily filter my search with this criteria. They don't include elevation or evacuation zone, and it takes a lot of manual digging to figure out which houses have been flipped or how they are constructed. This led me to build a thorough analysis if the St. Pete housing market. In the end, I found my house. I had to trade some of my nice-to-haves for the criteria that decreases the chances of my home being damaged. I decided to go with less square footage, for instance.

The data

Seven sources, joined at the parcel and zip level: county parcel records and sales history (PCPAO / FL DOR), building permits, Zillow & Redfin market metrics, Census ACS demographics, and FEMA flood-claim history.

The honest hard part wasn't analysis, it was reconciliation: these sources don't share keys, flood zones aren't a native parcel field (I spatial-joined FEMA FIRM maps to parcel lat/lon), and insurance has to be estimated per property. The figures below come from that joined dataset.

What the data showed

Asking price hides the real cost

Listings at the same sticker price diverge sharply once you compute true monthly cost (PITI plus insurance plus HOA/CDD). Insurance dominates: a ~$1,500/yr premium gap offsets a $50k price difference over a ten-year hold, which reorders "comparable" homes completely.

Cosmetic flips, priced like renovations

A flip score (days between purchase and relist, permit activity in that window, and price delta vs. zip comps) surfaces homes bought and re-listed within months at 2–3× replacement cost, with little real work permitted in between.

Flood & insurability quietly remove a big share of inventory

Applying the hard disqualifiers (FEMA flood zones, hurricane evac zones, roofs over 20 years, Federal Pacific / Zinsco panels, frame construction) eliminates a substantial fraction of listings before any deeper analysis, and the share varies a lot block to block.

Where momentum is actually building

A neighborhood-momentum signal (permit-issuance trend, days-on-market trend, price-cut rate, and ACS income growth) separates blocks that are heating up from those that just look active.

How I built it

Everything runs locally, with no cloud. Python scripts ingest each source into DuckDB, an in-process analytical database; dbt models turn the raw tables into the decision metrics above; a read-only FastAPI layer serves them; and a Next.js + MapLibre map-first UI puts every parcel on the map. DuckDB in-process means no infrastructure and fast iteration, the right call for a single-user decision tool.

What it told me

  • —The hard part of a data product isn't the model — it's joining messy public sources into something trustworthy. Parcel records, flood maps, and insurance quotes don't share keys, and reconciling them was most of the work.
  • —Encoding the actual decision — hard disqualifiers, true cost, insurance weighting — mattered more than any single metric. The tool was useful because it scored what I cared about, not a generic "estimate."
  • —DuckDB + dbt running locally is a genuinely great analytics stack: no infrastructure, fast iteration, and real transformation discipline without a warehouse bill.

Derived metrics

true_monthly_cost
PITI + insurance + HOA/CDD. The primary apples-to-apples cost comparison across listings.
flip_score
Days-to-relist + permit activity in that window + price delta vs. zip comp. Flags cosmetic flips priced at 2–3× replacement cost.
rental_exit_yield
Net yield after insurance, taxes, property management, and vacancy. Must be positive to qualify as a rental exit.
flood_risk_score
FEMA zone (spatial-joined onto parcels) plus NFIP claims history by zip.
neighborhood_momentum
Permit-rate trend + days-on-market trend + price-cut rate + ACS income growth.
